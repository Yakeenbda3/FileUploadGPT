<AnswerBox>
Yes. OpenAI states uploads are available on the web and in supported mobile apps, with the same
limits either way, since the caps belong to your account rather than your device. What you cannot do
on a phone is run a browser extension, because mobile Chrome does not support them at all.

## The limits are the same

Worth stating, because people assume mobile gets a smaller allowance.

| Limit | Phone | Desktop |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Storage | <Fact k="userStorageCap" /> | Same |

They are account limits, so the two devices share one pool. Three uploads on your phone in the
morning means none left on your laptop that afternoon, on the Free plan.

## Where phones are genuinely worse

**Getting at the file.** The document is often in email, or in a cloud drive, or on a computer
somewhere else. Getting it into the app is more steps than dragging it into a browser window.

**Photographing instead of uploading.** The camera is right there, so people photograph a page. That
works for one or two pages, since images are read directly. It does not work for a report, because a
phone photo of text is an image and not a text file, and there is a ceiling of about 100 images per
conversation with PDF pages counting toward it too.

**Long documents.** Anything needing to be split into parts is miserable to manage on a phone, and
the tools that automate splitting are browser extensions, which mobile cannot run.

<Callout variant="warn" title="Photographs of pages are images, not documents">
It is worth understanding why this fails on anything long. A photo goes in as a picture. ChatGPT can
read a picture, but the image ceiling is much lower than the text ceiling, so twenty pages
photographed is a very different proposition from twenty pages of text.

For anything substantial, get the actual file rather than photographing it.
</Callout>

## What works well on a phone

**A short document you already have.** A single PDF in your email, a text file in your cloud drive.
Straightforward.

**One or two pages photographed.** Reading a sign, a form, a page of a book. This is what the camera
is genuinely good for.

**A screenshot.** Fine, and often the quickest way to ask about something on screen.

**Continuing work you started on a computer.** The conversation is on your account, so a document
uploaded from your laptop is still there in the phone app.

## Extensions do not run on mobile

Chrome on Android and iOS does not support extensions. Not ours, not any of them.

This is a limitation of mobile Chrome rather than of any individual extension, and no product can
work around it. Anything promising a mobile browser extension is describing something that does not
exist.

## The sensible split

**Phone:** short documents, photographs of a page or two, screenshots, and continuing a conversation
you started elsewhere.

**Computer:** anything long, anything needing splitting, and anything where you want the whole
document read rather than the first part of it.

The conversation follows you between them, so starting on a laptop and continuing on a phone works
fine. It is only the getting-it-in step that wants a computer.
