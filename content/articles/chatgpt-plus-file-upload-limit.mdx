<AnswerBox>
Plus raises how many files you can send. It does not raise how large or how long any one of them can
be. The <Fact k="maxTextTokens" /> cap that truncates long documents is identical on every plan, so
if your problem is one very long file, upgrading will not fix it.
</AnswerBox>

## What you are actually buying

Two things, for uploads specifically.

**The daily restriction goes.** Free is capped at <Fact k="freeDailyUploads" />, which on a working
day is nothing. Plus replaces that with the general cap of <Fact k="rollingUploadRate" />, which for
most people is effectively unlimited.

**Project allowances double on Pro.** <Fact k="projectFilesPlus" /> on Plus, and
<Fact k="projectFilesPro" /> on Pro, Team, Education and Business. If you keep a working set of
reference documents in a Project, this is the row that decides between Plus and Pro.

## What you are not buying

<Callout variant="warn" title="Upgrading does not fix a truncated document">
Long documents get cut off at <Fact k="maxTextTokens" /> and this is the same number on every plan.
The upload succeeds, ChatGPT reads part of the file, and nothing warns you. Paying more does not
move that line even slightly.

If you upgraded specifically because a long report was being half read, you have not solved it, and
you may not have noticed because the summary still looks complete.
</Callout>

The same goes for scanned documents. Visual retrieval for PDFs exists only on Enterprise. On Plus,
as on Free, ChatGPT extracts the digital text and discards the images, so a scan yields nothing.

And storage is <Fact k="userStorageCap" /> across the board. If you hit "upload limit reached"
because you are full rather than because you are fast, Plus does not help with that either.

## Deciding whether to upgrade for uploads

**Upgrade if:** you send lots of separate files and keep running into the three-a-day wall on Free.
That is a real restriction and Plus removes it properly.

**Do not bother if:** your problem is a single long document being truncated, or a scanned PDF
producing nothing. Those are identical on both plans and no amount of money changes them.

For the truncation case, the fix is to send less at a time: convert to text, send only the section
you need, or split the document and send the parts in order with an instruction to hold the answer
until they have all arrived.

## One more thing about Plus and uploads

Failed upload attempts count toward the rate cap on every plan, including paid ones. On Plus you
have <Fact k="rollingUploadRate" /> so it rarely bites, but if you are batch-uploading and several
fail, you can reach a cap you did not think you were anywhere near.
