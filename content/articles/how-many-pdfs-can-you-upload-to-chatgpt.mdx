People ask this about PDFs in particular because PDFs are what they have a lot of: a folder of
research papers, a stack of invoices, four versions of the same contract. The count answer is
mildly boring. ChatGPT treats a PDF exactly like a text file or a spreadsheet, one slot each,
regardless of what is inside it.

The useful answer is the second half. Uploading ten PDFs and having ten PDFs read are different
achievements, and the limits that decide the second one are not counts at all.

## The counts, by plan

| Where you are | PDFs allowed | How it behaves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free, per day | <Fact k="freeDailyUploads" /> | Resets daily |
| Any paid plan | <Fact k="rollingUploadRate" /> | Rolls over three hours, no midnight reset |
| A project on Plus | <Fact k="projectFilesPlus" /> | Per project, not per chat |
| A project on Pro, Team, Business | <Fact k="projectFilesPro" /> | Per project, not per chat |
| A custom GPT | <Fact k="filesPerGpt" /> | For the lifetime of that GPT |
| One message | A small number | The interface says so if you exceed it |
| Storage behind all of it | <Fact k="userStorageCap" /> | Cumulative, never resets |

These stack rather than replacing each other. Having daily allowance left does not help inside a
project that is already full, and an empty project does not help if your storage is at the ceiling.

## Nothing on that table is about PDFs

There is no published PDF count anywhere, and that is worth understanding rather than just noting.
A three page PDF costs one slot. A nine hundred page PDF costs one slot. The caps count files.

Which cuts both ways. Merging six short PDFs into one before uploading turns six slots into one, and
on Free that is the difference between two days of allowance and part of an afternoon. But an
enormous PDF gets no extra room for being large, and it is the one most likely to be truncated.

<Callout variant="warn" title="Failed uploads count too">
OpenAI states that failed upload attempts can count toward the rate cap. A PDF that keeps failing
and gets retried three times has spent three slots and delivered nothing. On Free, with
<Fact k="freeDailyUploads" />, that is the whole day gone on one file that never arrived.
</Callout>

## The limit you actually meet first

Text and document files are capped at <Fact k="maxTextTokens" />. That is the ceiling that decides
how much of a PDF gets read, and it is nothing to do with how many you sent.

The failure mode is what makes it awkward. The PDF is not rejected. It uploads normally, ChatGPT
reads whatever fitted, and answers with total confidence about a document it has seen part of. No
warning, no marker in the reply.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Check how far it got">
Ask something only the ending can answer:

> What is the title of the final section, and what does its last paragraph say?

A vague answer means everything else you have been told is based on a fragment.
</Callout>

Size is the limit people expect and the one that almost never bites. The ceiling is
<Fact k="maxFileSize" />, and a text-based PDF of several hundred pages is usually a few megabytes.

## Scanned PDFs spend a slot and return nothing

Every plan except Enterprise handles documents as <Fact k="imagesInDocuments" />. So a scanned PDF,
which is pictures of pages rather than digital text, uploads successfully, counts against your
allowance, and yields no text to read.

Three of those on Free is your entire day for nothing. Check before you spend the slot: open the
PDF and try to select a sentence. If the cursor draws a box instead of highlighting words, it is a
scan and it needs OCR first.

## Projects and custom GPTs hold their files

The project caps are not per conversation. Files you added to a project in March are still sitting
in it, still counted, until you remove them. When a project refuses a new PDF, look at what is
already in there rather than at your daily allowance.

Custom GPTs are stricter and catch people out badly. <Fact k="filesPerGpt" />, for the lifetime of
that GPT, not per chat and not per month. If your GPT needs to know twenty documents, combine them
before you start rather than finding the ceiling at document eleven.

Underneath everything sits <Fact k="userStorageCap" />, shared across chats, projects and GPT
knowledge. PDFs are among the heaviest things people upload, so this fills faster with PDFs than with
anything else. Settings, then Storage, shows you where it stands. It shows <Fact k="quotaVisibility" />,
so there is no display anywhere for how much of the rolling upload rate is left.

## Stretching the allowance you have

**Merge before uploading.** Any PDF tool will combine several files into one. The caps count files,
so ten short reports become one slot.

**Send the pages you need.** Extracting chapter four into its own PDF costs the same one slot and
usually produces a better answer, because there is less irrelevant material competing for attention.

**Convert to text.** A .txt export of a PDF is a fraction of the size and drops the images that were
going to be discarded anyway on every plan except Enterprise. It does nothing for length, but it
removes size and image handling from the equation.

**Paste instead of uploading.** Pasted text is a message, not an upload, so it touches none of the
counts above. When you are completely out of uploads, this still works.

## What to check before you upload

The count is the easiest limit to reason about and usually the least relevant. Before you spend a
slot, two questions settle most of it.

Does the PDF contain selectable text, or is it a scan that will return nothing? And is it short
enough to fit inside <Fact k="maxTextTokens" />, or should you send the section you actually care
about instead of the whole thing?

Get those right and you will rarely notice the counts. Get them wrong and you can burn a day of
allowance on files ChatGPT never really read.
