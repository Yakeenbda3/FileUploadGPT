<AnswerBox>
It depends which limit you hit. The Free plan allows <Fact k="freeDailyUploads" />, and that is a
daily reset. The general cap is <Fact k="rollingUploadRate" />, and that one rolls: slots free up
gradually as older uploads age past three hours, rather than everything clearing at once. So waiting
an hour usually gets you some capacity back without waiting for any particular moment.
</AnswerBox>

## Rolling is not the same as resetting

This is the part that makes the limit feel unpredictable.

A daily reset is simple. Everything clears at a fixed time and you start again. The Free plan works
this way, with <Fact k="freeDailyUploads" />.

The general cap of <Fact k="rollingUploadRate" /> works differently. It looks backwards over the
last three hours and counts what happened in that window. There is no moment when the counter zeroes.
An upload you made two hours and fifty minutes ago drops out of the window ten minutes from now, and
that one slot comes back.

Practically, that means **you rarely have to wait the full three hours.** If you hit the cap because
of a burst of uploads twenty minutes ago, you will be waiting a while. If you have been uploading
steadily, some capacity returns within minutes.

<Callout variant="tip" title="What to actually do">
Wait an hour and try again, rather than waiting three. Most people who hit this hit it from a burst,
and a burst ages out of the window together.
</Callout>

## You cannot check how much is left

Worth knowing before you go looking, because the place people look is the wrong one.

ChatGPT shows Library storage usage under Settings, then Storage. That is your
<Fact k="userStorageCap" /> storage cap, which is about how much you are keeping, not how fast you
are uploading.

OpenAI states plainly that ChatGPT does not show how much of the rolling upload-rate quota has been
used or how much remains. There is no counter, no timer, and no indicator anywhere in the interface.

So if storage looks fine and you are still blocked, you are being held by the rate cap, and the only
signal you will get is that it starts working again.

## Failed attempts still count

The single most useful thing on this page.

OpenAI's documentation says failed upload attempts can count toward the rate cap. So a file that
keeps failing, that you keep retrying, is spending your allowance every time even though nothing
ever uploaded.

That is why people hit the limit having, from their point of view, uploaded almost nothing. They
uploaded one file eleven times.

<Callout variant="warn" title="If a file keeps failing, stop retrying it">
Work out why it failed first. A file that is too large will be too large the twelfth time. Retrying
is the one response guaranteed not to help, and guaranteed to cost you.
</Callout>

## The three limits people confuse

| Limit | Value | How it clears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free plan daily | <Fact k="freeDailyUploads" /> | Daily |
| Rolling rate | <Fact k="rollingUploadRate" /> | Gradually, as uploads age past 3 hours |
| Storage | <Fact k="userStorageCap" /> per user | Only when you delete chats holding files |

The third one never resets on its own. It is cumulative, shared across your chats, Projects and
custom GPT knowledge, and a file you uploaded in March is still occupying it today. If you are
blocked and storage is the reason, waiting will not help at any point, ever. You have to delete
something.

## Peak hours

OpenAI's documentation adds that these limits may be lowered during peak hours.

That means the number you hit today is not necessarily the number you will hit tomorrow, and a cap
that felt generous last week can feel tight now without anything having changed on your side. It
also means "I got 80 yesterday" is not a reason to expect 80 today.

## If you keep hitting it

Uploading fewer, larger things beats uploading many small ones, because the cap counts files rather
than bytes. Six files concatenated into one text document is one upload instead of six, and it
counts once.

That is also the reason splitting a document by hand can work against you: forty pasted chunks are
forty messages, not forty uploads, so pasting does not consume the upload cap at all. If you are
rate-limited, pasting text is the way through.
