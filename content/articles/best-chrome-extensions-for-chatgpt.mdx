## Check permissions before features

Do this first, every time, and it takes fifteen seconds.

An extension that runs on ChatGPT can read the page. That means your conversations, including
anything you have pasted into them. That is not sinister, it is how extensions work, and it is
exactly why the permission list matters.

On any Chrome Web Store listing, look for the permissions section.

| What it asks for | What to think |
| --- | --- |
| Read and change data on chatgpt.com | Expected. It has to touch the page to do anything. |
| Read and change data on all sites | Ask why. A ChatGPT tool has no business on your bank's site. |
| Read your browsing history | Almost never justified for this. |
| Communicate with cooperating websites | Means it talks to a server. Find out whose, and what it sends. |

<Callout variant="warn" title="The question worth asking">
Does this extension send my conversation anywhere? Some process everything locally in your browser.
Others send content to their own servers, which may be fine and may be exactly what you do not want
for work material. The privacy policy should say. If it does not, that is your answer.
</Callout>

Two more signals worth ten seconds each. When was it last updated, and does the developer have a
real website. ChatGPT's interface changes often, and an extension untouched for a year is probably
broken. An anonymous developer with no site and no support address is a support problem waiting to
happen.

## The categories, and what they are for

Most ChatGPT extensions do one of five things. Work out which problem you have before browsing.

**Getting large content in.** For when the document is longer than ChatGPT will take. These split it
into pieces and feed them in. Useful if you regularly hit the limit and pointless if you never do.
This is the category ours is in.

**Prompt management.** Saved prompts, templates, snippets you use constantly. Worth it if you type
the same instructions daily, and clutter if you do not.

**Exporting conversations.** Saving a chat as markdown, PDF, or a shareable page. Genuinely useful
if you need a record and mildly annoying otherwise.

**Search and organisation.** Finding an old conversation, adding folders and tags. The value scales
with how many chats you have. At forty it is transformative, at four it is noise.

**Interface changes.** Wider columns, different fonts, keyboard shortcuts. Pure preference, and the
lowest risk of the five, since they mostly restyle rather than read.

## Install one at a time

The habit that saves the most trouble.

Extensions that modify the same page can conflict, and when something breaks with four installed you
have no idea which one did it. Add one, use it for a week, then add the next.

It also settles the question that browsing cannot: whether you actually use it. Most extensions
people install are unused within a fortnight.

## Mobile, since it comes up constantly

Chrome on Android and iOS does not support extensions. Not ours, not any of them.

This is a limitation of mobile Chrome and there is no way around it. If you are reading this on a
phone and want any extension discussed here, you need a laptop or desktop. Send yourself the link
and it will be there when you get to a computer.

## A short checklist

1. What is the actual problem you keep hitting?
2. Does the permission list match that problem, or ask for far more?
3. Does the privacy policy say where your content goes?
4. Updated in the last few months?
5. Install one. Give it a week. Then consider the next.

Step two eliminates most of the bad ones on its own.
