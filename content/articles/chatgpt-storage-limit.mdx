## The limit that behaves differently from the others

Most ChatGPT upload limits are about speed. Too many files too quickly, wait a while, carry on.

Storage is not like that. It measures how much you are **keeping**, and it only goes down when you
delete things.

| Limit | Clears by |
| --- | --- |
| Free daily cap, <Fact k="freeDailyUploads" /> | Waiting a day |
| Rolling rate, <Fact k="rollingUploadRate" /> | Waiting about an hour |
| **Storage, <Fact k="userStorageCap" />** | **Deleting files. Nothing else** |

Which is why "I have not uploaded anything today and it says I hit my limit" is such a common and
such a confusing experience. Today is not what is being measured.

## It is shared, which is where it hides

The cap covers three places at once:

- Files in your ordinary **chats**
- Files in your **Projects**
- Files uploaded as knowledge to **custom GPTs** you built

People check their recent chats, see nothing large, and conclude it cannot be storage. Meanwhile a
custom GPT built months ago is holding <Fact k="filesPerGpt" /> worth of reference documents that
never went anywhere.

## Finding out where you stand

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Settings, then Storage">
    ChatGPT shows your Library usage here. This is the only upload quota it displays anywhere.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare against 25 GB">
    Close to it, and storage is your problem. Comfortably under, and something else is blocking you,
    almost certainly the rolling rate cap.
  </Step>
  <Step title="If it is storage, go hunting">
    Chats with large attachments first, then Projects, then any custom GPTs you built. The biggest
    single win is usually a chat where you uploaded a large file and never went back.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout variant="info" title="What it does not show">
OpenAI confirms ChatGPT does not display how much of the rolling upload-rate quota you have used or
have left. So if storage looks healthy and you are still blocked, there is nothing further to check.
You are rate limited, and waiting is the fix.
</Callout>

## What happens when you delete

Files live as long as the chat holding them. Delete the chat and the file goes with it.

OpenAI states that once you delete a chat, your account, or a custom GPT, the associated file is
removed from their systems within 30 days, unless it has already been de-identified or must be kept
for security or legal reasons.

The quota frees up for your purposes when you delete. The 30 days is about their systems, not about
when you can upload again.

## Keeping it from filling up

**Delete large chats when you finish with them.** The single largest contributor is usually a
handful of chats where you uploaded something big once.

**Prefer text over rich documents.** A Word file full of images is enormous. Its text is tiny. Since
images get discarded on every plan except Enterprise, uploading text costs you a fraction of the
space and loses nothing that was going to be read.

**Watch Projects and custom GPTs.** Files there persist by design, which is the point of them, and
means they quietly accumulate against your cap.

## If none of it adds up

If storage is well under the cap, you have not uploaded recently, and you are still being refused,
check status.openai.com before assuming it is your account. Uploads have their own incidents and the
message shown does not always distinguish one from a quota problem.
