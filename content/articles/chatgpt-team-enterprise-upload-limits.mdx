## The one genuine difference

OpenAI's File Uploads FAQ, on images inside documents:

> ChatGPT Enterprise supports Visual Retrieval for PDF files.
>
> All other plans and document files only support text-based retrieval. This means that ChatGPT will
> extract digital text from the file and discard any images.

This is the only upload capability on the list that a business plan adds, and it is Enterprise only.
Team does not get it. Business does not get it. Pro does not get it.

It matters more than it sounds. On every other plan a scanned contract produces nothing at all, the
chart carrying the number in a quarterly pack is simply absent, and a slide deck that makes its
argument in diagrams summarises down to a few headings. None of that is announced. The answer comes
back fluent and confident, built on whatever text survived.

<Callout variant="info" title="Enterprise is a procurement decision, not an upgrade button">
Enterprise is sold through OpenAI's sales process rather than bought from a settings page. If visual

OCR, and screenshotting figures to upload as images alongside the document, covers most of the same
ground for nothing.
</Callout>

## The numbers that do not move at all

<Fact k="maxFileSize" /> per file. <Fact k="maxTextTokens" /> per text or document file, with

Identical on Free, Plus, Pro, Team, Education, Business and Enterprise.

<Callout variant="warn" title="The expensive misunderstanding">
The token cap is what cuts long documents short, and it does not move for money. A 900 page policy
manual is truncated on Enterprise exactly as it is truncated on Free. The upload succeeds either way
and nothing tells you where the reading stopped.

If a team upgraded because long reports were coming back oddly thin, the upgrade did not address it,
and the summaries will carry on looking complete.
</Callout>

The rolling rate of <Fact k="rollingUploadRate" /> is unchanged too. Business plans lift the Free
plan restriction of <Fact k="freeDailyUploads" />, but so does Plus, and after that everybody is on
the same rate. Failed attempts count toward it on paid plans as well, which is worth knowing if your
team runs batch uploads.

## Storage once there is an organisation

Two published figures apply. <Fact k="userStorageCap" /> per end user, and
<Fact k="orgStorageCap" /> per organisation.

Do the arithmetic before planning around it. Four people at their individual ceiling already come to
the organisation figure, and ten people would come to 250 GB, so past a handful of seats the shared
pool is the one that runs out first. One person with a heavy Projects habit can quietly spend what
everyone else was going to use.

Storage also behaves unlike every other limit here. It is cumulative and it never clears on its own.
Rate caps sort themselves out within the hour. Storage only goes down when somebody deletes a chat,
a Project or a custom GPT, and on a shared account the material filling it often belongs to someone
who has left.

## Projects and custom GPTs on a shared account

The project allowance is the row that genuinely improves. <Fact k="projectFilesPlus" /> on Plus
against <Fact k="projectFilesPro" /> on Pro, Team, Education and Business. For a team keeping a
standing set of reference documents in one place, that doubling is the real purchase.

Custom GPTs do not improve. Knowledge is capped at <Fact k="filesPerGpt" /> for the lifetime of that
GPT, not per conversation, on every plan. A shared internal assistant built on twenty policy
documents does not fit, and no plan makes it fit. Concatenating related documents into one text file
before uploading is the usual answer, since the cap counts files rather than length.

## What the business plans change that is not a limit

Administration, mostly. Shared workspaces, seat management, retention controls and the usual
compliance apparatus. Real reasons to buy, all of them, and none of them touch a number in the table
above.

Data handling is the other one. OpenAI states it does not use content submitted to business
offerings such as the API and ChatGPT Enterprise to improve model performance. If the objection to
uploading company documents was about training rather than about size, that is the part of the
decision worth examining.

## Making the decision

**Buy Team or Business for** the shared workspace, the administration and the doubled project
allowance. Sensible reasons, all three.

**Buy Enterprise for** visual retrieval, if your documents genuinely are scans and charts, and for
the governance. Not for capacity.

**Do not buy any of them expecting** larger files, longer documents or faster uploads. Those
ceilings are the ones you already have. When a long document is the problem, the fix is to send less
at a time: convert it to plain text, send only the section that matters, or split it and send the
parts in order with an instruction to hold the answer until the last one arrives.
