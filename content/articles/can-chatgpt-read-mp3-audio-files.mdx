<AnswerBox>
Audio is not among the file types OpenAI lists for uploads. Their documentation describes what is
supported as &quot;<Fact k="supportedTypesStatement" />&quot; and audio appears in none of those four
families. Transcribe the recording and upload the transcript instead, which works well and is
usually more useful anyway.
</AnswerBox>

## The official position

OpenAI publishes no list of extensions. On supported types, the File Uploads FAQ says only:

> All common file extensions for text files, spreadsheets, presentations, and documents.

Audio is not one of the four. So the accurate statement is that audio is **not listed among the
supported upload types**, which is a fact about the documentation rather than a broader claim about
what the model can do.

## Voice mode is not file upload

This is where most of the confusion comes from, and it is a fair confusion.

ChatGPT can hold a spoken conversation. That is voice mode, and it works in real time through your
microphone. It is a different feature from attaching a file to a chat, and the fact that one exists
tells you nothing about the other.

If you have a recording sitting on your computer, voice mode is not the route to getting it
analysed.

## Transcribe, then upload

<Steps>
  <Step title="Get a transcript">
    Many meeting tools produce one automatically. Otherwise any transcription tool will do it, and
    most handle an hour of audio in a few minutes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save it as a text file">
    Plain .txt. Nothing to go wrong, and it is a fraction of the size of the audio.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload it">
    Now you can ask for a summary, the decisions taken, the action points, or anything else.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout variant="tip" title="Keep the speaker labels">
If your transcription tool marks who said what, keep those labels. It turns "summarise this" into
"what did each person commit to", which is usually the question you actually had.
</Callout>

## What a transcript loses

Worth being honest about, because it is not nothing.

Tone, hesitation, sarcasm, who sounded unconvinced, and how long a silence ran. If your question is
about how something was said rather than what was said, a transcript will not answer it and neither
would uploading the file, since that is not available either.

For everything else, content and decisions and detail, the transcript is genuinely better than the
audio: searchable, quotable, and possible to check.

## Long recordings

An hour of speech is roughly ten thousand words, which goes into ChatGPT without difficulty. A full
day of interviews, or a series of calls, can push past <Fact k="maxTextTokens" /> and get truncated
with no warning.

The check is the same as for any long document. Ask what the final section says. If it cannot tell
you, it did not read that far.
