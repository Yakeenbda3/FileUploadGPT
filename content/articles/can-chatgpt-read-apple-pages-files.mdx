Not usefully. A .pages, .numbers or .key file is an Apple container that stores its text as compressed
binary, so even when the upload goes through there is nothing readable to extract. Export first:

Apple's three iWork apps write three formats that almost nothing outside Apple reads. On a Mac you
never notice the seam. It shows up the moment the file has to go somewhere else, and ChatGPT counts
as somewhere else.

The fix is an export, and you already have the tool for it on a Mac, an iPhone or in any browser.

## What a .pages file actually is

Rename one to .zip and open it. Inside sits an Index folder full of .iwa files, a Data folder of
images, and a couple of small JPEG previews. Your writing is in those .iwa files, packed into
Apple's own binary format. Nothing outside iWork decodes them. Numbers and Keynote files are built
the same way.

That matters because of how uploads work. ChatGPT pulls the digital text out of a file and reads
that. Here there is no digital text to find, only a compressed structure it has no decoder for.

You can argue about whether Apple's formats count as common document, spreadsheet and presentation
types. The argument does not decide anything, because the technical answer already has: there is no
extractable text in the file.

<Callout variant="warn" title="Renaming to .zip does not rescue it">
ChatGPT can unpack an archive, but what comes out is still Apple binary. Documents made in iWork 09
and earlier sometimes include a QuickLook PDF preview inside the package, which is worth a look when
the original app is out of reach. Anything saved in the last decade carries only thumbnail images.
</Callout>

## Exporting on a Mac

All three apps use the same menu, and the submenu changes to match what the app can write.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the document and choose File, then Export To">
    Pages lists Word, PDF, EPUB, Plain Text and Rich Text Format. Numbers lists Excel, PDF, CSV and
    TSV. Keynote lists PowerPoint, PDF, Images, HTML and Movie.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pick the Microsoft equivalent">
    Word for Pages, Excel for Numbers, PowerPoint for Keynote. These preserve more usable text than
    a PDF of the same document.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the options in the dialog before you save">
    Keynote's PDF export has a layout setting. If your substance lives in presenter notes, choose the
    layout that includes them or they will not be in the file at all.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload the exported file">
    From here it behaves like any other document.
  </Step>
</Steps>

On iPhone and iPad the same thing lives behind the three dots button: tap it, choose Export, then
the format.

## Which export preserves the most text

| From | Export to | What you keep, what you lose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pages | Word (.docx) | Headings, lists, tables and footnotes survive. Plain text keeps the words but flattens tables and can drop anything in floating text boxes. |
| Numbers | Excel (.xlsx) | Every sheet and table stays in one file. CSV splits a multi table document into several files and keeps only values. |
| Keynote | PowerPoint (.pptx) | Slide text and presenter notes stay as text. A PDF turns the same deck into rendered pages. |

PDF is the honest fallback when an export mangles a complicated layout, and it is the right choice
when the arrangement on the page is the meaning. For plain reading and analysis, the Microsoft
formats give ChatGPT more to work with.

For a Pages document that is nothing but prose, plain text is perfectly good and much smaller. The
moment there are tables, captions or sidebars, use Word instead.

## When you do not own a Mac

The usual version of this problem: a colleague emailed a .pages file and you are on Windows.

Open iCloud.com in any browser and sign in with a free Apple Account. Go to Pages, drag the file into
the window to upload it, open it, then use the More menu, the three dots, and choose Download a Copy.
Pick Word or PDF. Numbers and Keynote work identically on the same site.

Faster, if the sender is reachable: ask them to export. It costs them fifteen seconds, and it avoids
sending a document you may not own through anything else.

<Callout variant="info" title="About online converters">
They work. They also mean uploading the document to a company you have no relationship with. For a
draft blog post, fine. For a contract or anything with personal data in it, use iCloud or ask the
sender.
</Callout>

## What the export does not fix

Images. Once your file is a DOCX, PPTX or XLSX, ChatGPT reads the text and discards the pictures.
The current behaviour is <Fact k="imagesInDocuments" />.

So a Keynote deck built from diagrams and screenshots exports cleanly and still reads as almost
nothing: a list of slide titles with the substance missing. Same for a Pages document holding scanned
pages. If that describes your file, you need the words themselves, which means either retyping them
or running OCR before there is anything to upload.

Numbers charts behave better than you might expect, because the numbers behind a chart are real cell
data and travel to Excel intact. The chart itself does not, and does not need to.

## After the export, the ordinary limits apply

Nothing about iWork changes the caps. A file can be up to <Fact k="maxFileSize" />, text and document
files are capped at <Fact k="maxTextTokens" />, and spreadsheets sit outside that token cap with a
practical ceiling near <Fact k="maxSpreadsheetSize" />.

The length cap is the one that catches people, because passing it is silent. The upload succeeds,
ChatGPT reads as far as it can, and nothing announces the cut. A book length Pages manuscript
exported to Word is exactly the kind of file that hits this. Ask what the final chapter is called. If
it cannot tell you, it did not get there.

## The decision

Do not upload iWork files. Export Pages to Word, Numbers to Excel and Keynote to PowerPoint, use PDF
only when the layout carries the meaning, and use iCloud.com when there is no Mac in reach. Then
check whether the document was mostly pictures, because that is the failure the export cannot solve.
