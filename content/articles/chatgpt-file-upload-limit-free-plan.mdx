Only the first row is a Free plan restriction. Everything below it applies on every plan including

OpenAI also notes that limits may be lowered during peak hours, so three is the number to plan
around rather than the number you are promised.

## Three a day runs out faster than you expect

Not because three is unreasonable, but because of what counts.

**Failed attempts count.** OpenAI's documentation says failed upload attempts can count toward the
cap. On a plan with three, a single file that fails twice before working has consumed your day.

**Every file counts separately.** Three documents is three, even if they are small and related.

<Callout variant="warn" title="On Free especially, do not retry a failing upload">
With three slots, retrying a stubborn file costs your entire allowance for the day and produces
nothing. Work out why it failed first. If it is too large or too long, it will fail identically
every time.
</Callout>

## What still works when you have run out

This is the part worth knowing, and it is not obvious.

**Pasting is not uploading.** The daily cap counts file uploads. Text you paste into the message box
is an ordinary message, and it does not touch the upload allowance at all.

So when you are out of uploads, this still works:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the document on your computer">
    Word, a text editor, a PDF reader, anything.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy the text">
    Select all, copy. For a PDF, this only works if it has real text rather than being a scan.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Paste it into ChatGPT">
    For anything long, in several parts, telling ChatGPT first that more are coming and to wait
    before answering.
  </Step>
</Steps>

It is more manual than uploading. It also completely sidesteps the limit that is stopping you, which
is worth the inconvenience when the alternative is waiting until tomorrow.

## Making three uploads go further

**Bundle before uploading.** Several documents concatenated into one text file is one upload rather
than several. For code especially, one file with a header line before each source file preserves
everything useful and costs a single slot.

**Convert to plain text first.** Smaller, faster, more reliable, and it sidesteps the size ceiling
entirely on anything text-heavy.

**Send only the section you need.** You rarely need all three hundred pages read. One chapter is one
upload and usually produces a better answer, because there is less irrelevant material competing for
attention.

## What upgrading changes

Worth being precise, because the marketing is not.

**It does change:** the three-per-day restriction goes, replaced by the general cap of
<Fact k="rollingUploadRate" />. Project allowances rise to <Fact k="projectFilesPlus" /> on Plus and
<Fact k="projectFilesPro" /> on Pro, Team, Education and Business.

**It does not change:** the <Fact k="maxTextTokens" /> cap that truncates long documents, the
<Fact k="maxFileSize" /> ceiling, or the <Fact k="userStorageCap" /> storage cap. Nor does it change
the rule that images in documents are discarded on every plan except Enterprise, which is why a
scanned PDF fails identically on Free and on Pro.

So: if your problem is **how many** files, paying helps. If your problem is **one file that is too
long**, paying changes nothing at all, and that is the more common problem.
