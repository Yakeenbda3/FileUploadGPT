<AnswerBox>
A large PowerPoint is large because of the parts ChatGPT throws away. Photos, screenshots and
embedded video account for nearly all the weight of a heavy deck, and every plan except Enterprise
extracts the text and discards the images. Compress the media, or export the slide text and speaker
notes into a document and upload that instead. Either route costs you nothing ChatGPT was going to
read.
</AnswerBox>

The hard ceiling is <Fact k="maxFileSize" /> per file, which sounds generous until you meet a deck
built from full-bleed photography with a couple of clips embedded in it. The words in that same
deck, speaker notes included, usually come to a few tens of kilobytes.

That gap is the whole story. Fixing a PowerPoint upload is mostly a matter of separating the part
that weighs something from the part that gets read.

## Where the megabytes actually are

A .pptx file is a zip archive. Inside it sits a small pile of XML describing the slides, and a media
folder holding every image, video and audio file in the deck at its original resolution. That
includes the ones sitting behind other objects, the ones on unused layouts, and the parts of photos
you cropped away on the slide, which PowerPoint keeps by default in case you want them back.

| Part of the deck | Carries the file weight | Read by ChatGPT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Photos and screenshots | Yes | No |
| Embedded video and audio | Yes | No |
| Charts, diagrams, icons | Some | No |
| Slide titles and body text | Barely | Yes |
| Text inside tables | Barely | Usually |
| Speaker notes | Barely | Usually |

Everything in the top half is what makes the file too big. Everything in the bottom half is what
ChatGPT would have answered from. There is almost no overlap, which is why this problem is easier
than it looks.

## Compress the media and re-upload

The fastest fix, and the one to try first if the deck is close to the limit rather than far past it.

PowerPoint on Windows has this built in. Select any picture, open the Picture Format tab, choose
Compress Pictures, then tick both Delete cropped areas of pictures and Apply to all pictures in this
file. Embedded video and audio are handled separately, under File and then Info, as Compress Media.
On Mac the equivalent lives under File and then Reduce File Size.

Decks routinely lose most of their size this way. Retrieval from documents is <Fact k="imagesInDocuments" />,
so the pictures were never going to be read, and dropping them to screen resolution costs you
nothing at all in the answer you get. Keep a copy of the original if the deck still has to be
presented.

## Export the text and the notes instead

The better fix when the deck is genuinely enormous, or when you want the analysis to be about the
content rather than the file.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Send the deck to Word, with notes">
    On Windows, File, then Export, then Create Handouts, and pick the layout with notes below the
    slides. You get a Word document containing slide text and speaker notes in order. On Mac, print
    the Notes layout to PDF for the same result.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save it as a document or plain text">
    A .docx or .txt file of the same deck is normally a few tens of kilobytes. There is no size
    problem left to solve at that point.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tell ChatGPT what it is looking at">
    Say that this is the text and speaker notes from a slide deck, that the visuals are not
    included, and that the notes carry the argument. Without that framing it treats sparse slide
    text as the whole presentation.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout variant="warn" title="A sparse deck produces a confidently thin summary">
Twelve slides carrying a chart and four words each will summarise as roughly fifty words of content,
delivered as though that were the presentation. Nothing in the answer indicates that most of the
meaning was in the pictures. This is worth knowing before you judge the output.
</Callout>

## What you lose, and how to get part of it back

Three things do not survive, and each has a workaround if it matters.

**Charts.** A chart on a slide is an image, so the figures in it are gone. Paste the underlying
numbers into the chat as text or as a small table. You get a better answer from the raw numbers than
from any description of the picture.

**Diagrams and process flows.** These carry structure that the slide text does not restate. Write
the sequence out in one line each. It takes a minute and it is usually the missing piece.

**Design, layout and hierarchy.** Nothing textual to work with here at all. Export the specific
slides as images and upload those, capped at <Fact k="maxImageSize" /> each. This is the only route
to feedback on how a slide looks. There is more detail in the piece on
[what ChatGPT reads from a presentation](/blog/chatgpt-powerpoint-pptx-support).

## When the deck is long rather than heavy

A different problem with the same symptoms, and the one people misdiagnose.

Slide text is light, so a single deck almost never approaches the <Fact k="maxTextTokens" /> length
cap. A year of weekly decks concatenated into one file, or a training set with dense notes on every
slide, can. When that happens there is no error. The file uploads, and ChatGPT answers from as much
of it as it could hold.

Check it directly. Ask what the final slide is titled, or what the last section covers. If it cannot
say, it never got there, and the summary you were given describes the opening only.

## The short version

Too many megabytes? Compress the pictures. Images are discarded on upload anyway, so you lose
nothing that was going to be read.

Want a proper analysis? Export the slide text and speaker notes to a document and upload that, then
say plainly that the visuals are missing.

Need the visuals looked at? Export those slides as images. That is the only way the design and the
charts get seen at all.
